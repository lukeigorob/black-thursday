# B-17 Crew Gear: 8th Air Force, 14 October 1943 (Schweinfurt)

Research notes for dressing and equipping the game's 3D crewmen. Scope: US Eighth Air Force B-17F/early B-17G crews, England, autumn 1943.

## Confidence levels
- **VERIFIED**: a museum, primary document or period photo caption states it.
- **PROBABLE**: good secondary or collector sources agree, or it follows directly from verified dates.
- **UNVERIFIED**: a plausible inference, a single dealer claim, or my estimate. Every hex colour is UNVERIFIED: each one is an estimate from surviving examples and period colour film, not a specification.

## 1. Flying clothing

### B-3 jacket and A-3 trousers (sheepskin)
- The B-3 is a heavy sheepskin (shearling) jacket, with the wool inside, the brown-dyed suede or leather outside, a large shearling collar and leather belts and straps at the collar. Some examples have two-tone leather panels at the pockets, trim and shoulders. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- The A-3 trousers are the matching sheepskin bib trousers. In 1943 photos they are worn over the F-1 heated suit or the A-4 coverall, with the B-3 on top. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- The sets were heavy and bulky, but in 1943 they were still the warmest outer layer and the main fallback when a heated suit failed. **PROBABLE** [303-43], [ARROW]
- Colours: outer leather is a medium-to-dark brown that wears paler and patchy at the elbows and seams, about `#5A3A22` (worn `#7A5534`). Fleece is cream to off-white, about `#E6DAC0`, greying with use to `#CFC3A8`. **UNVERIFIED**

### Electrically heated suits
- **F-1 "Blue Bunny"**: a one-piece, 24-volt, series-wired coverall built for the B-17, worn under the outer flying clothing. NMUSAF and the Museum of Flight both use the "Blue Bunny" name. **VERIFIED** [NMUSAF-F1], [MOF-F1], [ARROW]
  - The wires often broke with flexing, and one break could kill the whole suit. NMUSAF notes the broken wires could also shock the wearer. **VERIFIED** [NMUSAF-F1], [ARROW]
  - It came with E-1 heated gloves that plug into the sleeve and D-1 heated shoe inserts ("24 volts D.C.") worn inside the A-6 boot. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
  - 303rd BG caption for 13 September 1943: "All of the crews at this time would have had the F-1 heated suit." **VERIFIED** (period-photo commentary) [303-F2]
  - Colour: a medium blue wool/cotton twill, about `#3E5A8A` (faded `#5B7099`). The suit's name confirms it was blue; the exact shade is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **F-2**: standardized August 1943. It has four pieces: an OD wool-blanket heated jacket and trousers (parallel wired), worn under an unheated outer jacket and trousers. **PROBABLE** [ARROW], [303-F2]
  - The outer layer looks like an ordinary OD uniform: wool gabardine for officers, serge for enlisted. **PROBABLE** [303-F2]
  - The F-2A with thermostats came in February 1944. **PROBABLE** [ARROW]
  - Dated F-2 photos on the 303rd site are from 1944 (6 February, 23 March). I found no photo of an F-2 in combat in the ETO before 14 October 1943. **Treat it as rare or absent on 14 Oct 1943.** UNVERIFIED
- **F-3**: a lighter two-piece set, with a short bolero-style jacket and bib trousers, using improved flex-proof wire. The American Air Museum describes crews upgrading "to F-2 and F-3 suits in 1944." **1944 only. PROBABLE** [ARROW], https://www.americanairmuseum.com/media/40735
- 8th AF practice in 1943 was to wear the F-1 under sheepskin, an A-4 coverall or a B-10 jacket. Fleming (waist gunner, 6 July 1943) puts the heated suit over heavy underwear, then heated shoes, coveralls with a Mae West, heated gloves with wool gloves over them, and the parachute. **VERIFIED** [303-DRESS]

### A-2 leather jacket
- Brown horsehide or goatskin, with a knit waistband and cuffs, shoulder epaulets and a snap-flap collar. It was officially superseded in 1943 but stayed in very wide use. **VERIFIED** [EDUARD], [303-43]
- Two approved colours: seal (dark brown) about `#3B2618` and russet about `#7B4A2A`. **PROBABLE** [EDUARD]; hexes UNVERIFIED
- It was worn on missions in 1943 over the F-1 and under the Mae West and harness. 303rd radioman Vieira was photographed in one on 13 September 1943. A 303rd recreation of a "first year" officer shows a crusher cap, HS-33 headset, T-30 throat mic, A-2, B-4 vest (dated 20 May 1943), E-1 heated gloves under A-9 gloves, and an AN-6513-1 chute. **VERIFIED** (dated photos) [303-43], [303-F2]
- On its own, an A-2 is not warm enough at -40 °C. Over a working F-1 in the cockpit or nose it was common. **PROBABLE**

### Coveralls and other outer garments
- The A-4 flying suit (a lightweight gabardine coverall) was worn over the F-1 and sometimes under the A-3 trousers. Clark Gable was photographed in an A-4 with a B-6 helmet. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- The B-10 jacket (OD cotton shell, alpaca/wool pile lining, mouton collar) appears in 8th AF photos from 1943 into 1944. **PROBABLE** [303-HEAD], [303-CHUTE]
- The AN-S-31 summer suit was standardized in 1943. **PROBABLE** [EDUARD]
- Colours: A-4/AN-S-31 khaki-OD about `#7A6F4E`; B-10 shell OD about `#6A6040` with a brown mouton collar about `#5A4632`. **UNVERIFIED**

### Who wore what
- Waist gunners stood at open windows in a slipstream at -30 to -45 °C. They had the most frostbite cases and wore the heaviest layers: the F-1 under the full B-3/A-3 sheepskin set, or the F-1 under an A-4 or B-10 with A-3 trousers. **PROBABLE** [AACM], [DTIC-COLD], [ARROW]
- DTIC excerpt: in the 14 months to December 1943, cold casualties among 8th AF airmen exceeded combat wounds, with waist gunners the most exposed. **PROBABLE** (excerpt only, PDF not read) [DTIC-COLD]
- Pilot, co-pilot, navigator and bombardier were less exposed to wind blast and often wore lighter outer layers: an A-2 or B-10, or an A-4 coverall over the F-1. **PROBABLE**
- One source says the tail and ball gunners had first claim on the B-17's heated-suit power, and waist gunners came next if power allowed. **UNVERIFIED** (single collector page) [ARROW]

## 2. Head

### Leather and sheepskin helmets
- **B-6**: a winter helmet of brown shearling (fleece inside) with a chin strap. Most were issued without earcups, and units added rubber receiver cups or HS-38 cushions, plus snaps for the mask. It was in heavy use throughout 1943 and stayed in use into 1944. **VERIFIED** [303-HEAD]
- **A-9**: a summer helmet with sewn-on receiver cups. Its use peaked in 1943. **VERIFIED** [303-HEAD]
- **AN-H-16**: a winter helmet, rare. It looks like the A-11 but has a shearling interior and a small brow bill. **VERIFIED** [303-HEAD]
- **A-11**: an intermediate leather helmet with built-in rubber earphone mountings for ANB-H-1 receivers. It was standardized on 6 August 1943. **PROBABLE** [EDUARD] and dealer boilerplate. Few would have reached England by 14 October 1943, and dated A-11 photos on the 303rd site are 1944. **UNVERIFIED: mostly 1944**
- The RAF Type C helmet was used by some AAF bomber crew, with ANB-H-1 receivers often taped into the earcups. **VERIFIED** [303-HEAD]
- Colours: B-6 leather about `#5C3B24`, fleece edge `#D9C9A8`; A-11 leather a darker, smoother brown, about `#4A2E1C`. **UNVERIFIED**

### Earphones
- **ANB-H-1 receivers**: about 2.5 in (6 cm) across, black Bakelite, fitted in black or brown rubber "doughnut" cups (sewn onto B-6/A-9 helmets, or moulded into the A-11). An HS-33/HS-38 headset is the same receivers on a sprung headband, with small black cushions or larger brown ones. **VERIFIED** [303-HEAD], dealer listings
- Colours: cups about `#1E1A18` (black) or `#5A3A28` (brown). **UNVERIFIED**

### Officers' service caps ("50-mission crush")
- The 303rd notes the crusher cap was worn with an HS-33 headset "in the first year in England," with the wire stiffener pulled out so the headset sat over it. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- At altitude in combat in October 1943: an A-10/A-10R mask hangs from its own head harness, so a cap and headset can be worn with it. Pilots in the warmer cockpit are photographed this way. **PROBABLE** for some pilots and co-pilots. **UNVERIFIED** as typical. I found no dated October 1943 cockpit photo. Safe choice: pilot in a B-6 or A-9 with the mask clipped on, co-pilot optionally in a crusher cap with headset.

### Steel helmets
- In 1943 crews wore M1 infantry helmets over their flying helmets with the liner removed. **VERIFIED** [303-43], [RAFM-M3]
- From January 1943 the 306th BG hammered out the sides of M1s and cut the liners so they fitted over earphones. **VERIFIED** (unit history) [306-HELM]
- Grow helmet: steel strips between brown leather and a chamois lining, about 0.9 kg, worn over a flying helmet with cut-outs at the ears. 19,000 were ordered from Wilkinson Sword in mid-1943 and production was slow. It became the T3 and then the M4, standardized December 1943. **VERIFIED** [IWM-GROW], [IWM-GROW2]
- M3: an M1 with cut-out sides, hinged armoured ear flaps and flocked OD paint. The RAF Museum says "adopted 1943"; the 306th says standard issue in early 1944; a dealer says it reached the ETO in December 1943. **VERIFIED (design) / PROBABLE (Dec 1943 or later)** [RAFM-M3], [306-HELM]
- **For 14 Oct 1943**: no M3s or M4s yet. A few men (waist, radio, nose) might wear a cut-down M1, and a very few a British-made Grow helmet. Turret gunners did not, because there was no room. **PROBABLE**
- Colours: M1 OD paint about `#4B4E36`; Grow helmet brown leather about `#6B4A2E`. **UNVERIFIED**

## 3. Oxygen

- **A-9**: the "short" mask, Spec. 3125 (January 1942). Its use peaked in 1943. **VERIFIED** [303-HEAD]
- **A-10, A-10 Revised, A-10A** (Acushnet): demand masks. The 303rd says A-10-family use "peaked in 1943." A dealer reports an A-10 Revised dated April 1943. A museum record says the Acushnet masks had fit problems and iced up at altitude. **VERIFIED (peak 1943)** [303-HEAD]; **PROBABLE** for the rest
- **A-14** (Ohio Chemical / Bulbulian design): standardized July 1943 and made from spring 1943. The 303rd says it was used by bomber crews "from early 1944." By the 1944 manual it is standard issue and the A-10 and A-10 Revised are obsolete. **VERIFIED** [SURV44], [303-HEAD]
- **Standard on 14 Oct 1943**: the **A-10 Revised** (some plain A-10 and A-9 still in use). The A-14 is at most a rarity. **PROBABLE**
- **Appearance**: the A-10 family is a rubber face-piece covering the nose and mouth, with elastic head straps and a short corrugated hose to the rapid-disconnect fitting. The microphone is either a T-30 throat mic or a mic in the mask. Colour is dark olive green to near black, about `#2F3326`, with a black hose `#1C1C1A`. Colour **UNVERIFIED**.
- **A-14 appearance (for 1944)**: grey-green rubber about `#6E7466`, a nose bridge, a chin "valve" housing, and an ANB-MC-1 microphone. **PROBABLE**; hex **UNVERIFIED**
- **Hose and connection**: the mask hose plugs through a rapid-disconnect into the hose from an A-12 demand regulator at the crew station. It should take 12 lb of pull to separate. Above 24,000 ft the collar must be screwed up tight. **VERIFIED** [SURV44]
- **Regulators**: the A-12 demand regulator at the station (Auto-Mix lever). A walk-around bottle with an A-13 regulator for moving around. **VERIFIED** [SURV44], [303-FLAK]
- **Wear with helmets**: the A-10A and A-14 are suspended from clips or snaps on the winter and summer helmets. The B-6 had snaps added to fasten the mask, though the 303rd warns these pulled out. **VERIFIED** [SURV44], [303-HEAD]

## 4. Goggles

- **B-7**: an Air Corps goggle with a split lens and a sponge-padded frame. It appears in 303rd photos of January 1943. **VERIFIED** [303-43], [303-HEAD]
- **AN6530**: a 1943 strengthened B-7 (NASM: "essentially" the same), with amber or green lenses and a single-piece face pad. Head straps came in white, grey or black. **VERIFIED** [NASM-6530], [WIKI-6530], [303-HEAD]
- **B-8**: a rubber-framed one-piece-lens goggle with interchangeable Polaroid lenses. One source says late 1943; another says standardized in 1944, making the AN6530 obsolete. **PROBABLE: rare to absent in Oct 1943** [WIKI-6530]
- **RAF Mk VII**: "many crews wore them in 1943," often with flip shields. **VERIFIED** [303-HEAD]
- **How worn**: mostly pushed up on the forehead over the helmet and pulled down only for glare, fire or wind. Waist gunners at the open windows wore them down. **PROBABLE**
- Colours: AN6530/B-7 frame chrome-black about `#2A2A2A`; lenses amber `#C8902A` or green `#3F5B3A`; pad tan `#8A6C4C`. **UNVERIFIED**

## 5. Gloves and boots

- The usual pattern was a rayon or silk liner, then a heated glove, then a leather glove or mitten over it. Fleming (6 July 1943) wore wool gloves over his heated gloves. The 303rd notes an enlisted man in rayon gloves under heated gloves. **VERIFIED** [303-DRESS], [303-F2]
- **E-1 heated gloves**: plug into the F-1 sleeve. They were worn with A-9 gloves over them. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- RAF heated gloves were issued to American crews during a shortage, and RAF 1941-pattern gloves appear in 1943 photos. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- **A-9**: a leather flying mitten or glove with shearling lining. **PROBABLE**
- **A-10**: an intermediate leather glove, standardized 20 July 1938 and used throughout the war. **PROBABLE** [EDUARD]
- **A-6 boot**: moulded rubber sole, brown suede/leather upper, shearling lining, zip front. It was standardized 27 March 1937 and "typically worn over an electrically heated insert or a low-quarter shoe." **VERIFIED** [DPLA-A6]
- D-1 heated shoe inserts were worn inside the A-6. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- F-2 felt boots belong with the F-2 suit and are later. **PROBABLE**
- Colours: A-6 suede about `#6E4E36`, black sole `#1F1C1A`, fleece edge `#D9CBAE`. A-9/A-10 gloves brown `#5A3B26`. **UNVERIFIED**

## 6. Flak vests

- **Origin**: Col. Malcolm Grow (8th AF Surgeon) and Wilkinson Sword. The first 12 sets went into combat testing in March 1943 and the first 600 were made in England before US production started. **VERIFIED** [IWM-VEST], [AFMS]
- **Numbers**: by 1 January 1944, 13,500 suits had been produced. **VERIFIED** [AFMS] I found no figure for issue as of October 1943.
- **Training film**: a 1943 USAAF film, "Flak Suit," shows a B-17 gunner being fitted and the pilot and co-pilot wearing armour. **VERIFIED** (film description) [FF-FILM]
- **Who**: gunners, navigators, bombardiers and radio operators. Ball turret and upper turret gunners had none. **VERIFIED** [IWM-VEST]
- **For 14 Oct 1943**: the vests were in service and many groups had some, but issue was patchy, not one per man. Crews often put them on only for the target run or flak. **PROBABLE** [IWM-VEST], [AFMS], [FF-FILM]
- **Design**: 2-inch manganese steel squares in individual pockets, covered in heavy duck cloth. The front and back join at the shoulders, with a belt over the upper abdomen and an apron (M3 / M4) below. **VERIFIED** [IWM-VEST], [303-FLAK]
  - A single **red pull strap** releases the shoulder connections and waist belt so the whole suit drops. **VERIFIED** [NMUSAF-PROT], [303-FLAK]
  - British-made sets have a mid-brown duck cover; US-made sets are dark green. **VERIFIED** [IWM-VEST]
  - The M1 is the full vest with back armour; the M2 is the "sitting" version without back armour; the M3 apron hangs from the vest. **PROBABLE** [303-FLAK] (citing C.G. Sweeting)
  - Weight: about 7 kg [IWM-VEST]; 16 lb [AFMS]; 25 lb 10 oz for a full set [303-FLAK]. **VERIFIED** as stated, but the sources conflict.
- Colours: British brown duck about `#7A6446`; US dark green `#4A4A32`; red strap `#A82820`. **UNVERIFIED**

## 7. Mae West life preservers

- **B-3**: a cloth outer cover with rubber bladders. It is narrower and more flexible than the B-4, has a square leather patch on the front, and the example shown was made September 1942. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- **B-4**: "all rubber and more rigid," made from February 1943 and the replacement for the B-3. 1943-dated examples are Goodyear (inspected 3 May 1943) and Hodgman (20 May 1943). It carries a dye marker, two CO2 cartridges, pull strings and red hoses. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- MOTAT's c.1943 B-4 is a yellow/khaki cotton halter hung round the neck, with rubber bladders, inflated by a CO2 bomb or an oral tube. **VERIFIED** [MOTAT]
- A B-4 is described as yellow rubberized canvas. **PROBABLE** [EDUARD]
- **Shape**: a horseshoe or collar of two bladders on the chest, meeting behind the neck; a low back panel and waist strap; crotch/leg straps; CO2 cylinders and pull cords at the lower front corners; oral tubes at the upper chest. From the back you see a thin yellow collar band across the shoulders and a waist strap; it is mostly hidden under the parachute harness. **PROBABLE**
- On 14 Oct 1943 both the B-3 and B-4 were in use, with the B-4 the more common. Both were worn under the parachute harness. **VERIFIED** [303-CHUTE]
- Stencils: black "VEST LIFE PRESERVER TYPE B-4" and the maker's data in small type on the front. **UNVERIFIED**
- Colours: about `#D2A82E` (chrome yellow, weathering to mustard `#B8923A`), with red inflation tubes `#9E2A22`. **UNVERIFIED**

## 8. Parachutes

- **QAC (Quick Attachable Chest)**: the man wears a light harness with a back pad; the chest pack is clipped on with two large hooks to D-rings only when he needs it. Types AN-6513-1 and AN-6513-1A; dated examples from February to September 1943. **VERIFIED** [303-CHUTE], [IWM-QAC]
- A dealer quotes AAF records that QACs were 81% of parachutes on B-17s, B-24s and B-26s from April 1943 to September 1944. **PROBABLE** (dealer quoting records)
- **A-3 QAC harness**: OD webbing, the same large hooks, dated from 22 September 1943. **VERIFIED** [303-CHUTE] An A-4 QAC harness is later (1944). **PROBABLE**
- The RAF observer harness was issued to some American crews in 1943. **VERIFIED** [303-43]
- **Back packs**: the B-7 (early 1943), and the B-8, which "appeared at the front lines of the 8th AF around December 1943." **VERIFIED** [303-43], [303-CHUTE]
- **Seat pack**: the AN-6510 (S-1) was used by pilots throughout the war. Seat packs appear in 8th AF depot shop photos. **PROBABLE** [EDUARD], [303-CHUTE] B-17 pilots in 1943 wore a QAC harness or a B-7 back pack. **UNVERIFIED** which was more common.
- **Chest pack location**: stowed near the station (on the floor, in a rack or behind the seat) and clipped on only in an emergency. Gunners and the nose crew wore only the harness. **PROBABLE**
- **Ball turret**: there was no room for a parachute in the turret. The gunner had to rotate the turret, climb into the fuselage and clip on his chute there. **PROBABLE** [CRADLE]
- **Red harness paint**: TO 13-5-39 of May 1944 required red markings. On 14 October 1943 harnesses are plain OD, with no red. **VERIFIED** [303-CHUTE]
- **Hardware**: two large snap hooks with D-rings on the chest; a ripcord handle and pocket on the pack; leg-strap snaps. The finish is OD paint or bare steel. **PROBABLE** [303-CHUTE], [IWM-QAC]
- Colours: OD webbing `#6B6340`; khaki canvas pack `#8C7C56`; hardware `#9A9A94` / `#4A4A3A`. **UNVERIFIED**

## 9. Equipment worn on the body

- **T-30 throat microphone**: two black rubber pickups about 1 in (2.5 cm) across, worn against the throat on an elastic neck strap, with a cord to a PL-291 plug. **PROBABLE** (dealer listings; also shown in [303-43])
- **Interphone and power**: each station had a jack box (BC-366) for the two comm lines, a heated-suit rheostat and an A-12 regulator. Each man has a headset cord, a mic cord, a heated-suit cord (2 ft pigtail plus 6 ft extension) and an oxygen hose trailing from him to the wall. **VERIFIED** [303-FLAK], [ARROW]
- **Walk-around bottle**: a small yellow cylinder with an A-13 regulator, lasting minutes. The engineer had the larger D-2. **VERIFIED** [SURV44], [303-FLAK]
- **Bail-out bottle**: the H-2 holds about 12 minutes of oxygen, with a bayonet fitting to the A-14. Its widespread 8th AF use is 1944. **PROBABLE** [SURV44]; for 1943 **UNVERIFIED**
- Each harness carried a first-aid kit with a morphine syrette and tourniquet. **VERIFIED** [303-CHUTE]
- Colours: yellow bottle `#C9A23A`; black cords `#1A1A1A`. **UNVERIFIED**

## 10. Officers and enlisted

- **Officers** (second or first lieutenants, sometimes captains on lead crews): pilot, co-pilot, navigator and bombardier. **Enlisted** (sergeants, staff and technical sergeants): engineer/top turret, radio operator, ball, two waists and tail. **VERIFIED** (standard B-17 crew organization)
- **Insignia in flight**: almost nothing shows once the outer layers are on. Officers wear bars on the shirt collar or on the A-2/B-10 epaulets, and these are usually covered by the Mae West and harness. Enlisted chevrons are on the shirt sleeves, under the flying clothing. **PROBABLE**
- **Painted jacket backs**: painted A-2s with aircraft art and mission bombs exist from 8th AF units in 1943. Many tallies were painted only after the veteran got home. **PROBABLE** [303-43] and dealer pages. On combat gear they were mostly on A-2s, and hidden under the harness.
- **Patches**: squadron patches (left breast) and the 8th AF shoulder patch on A-2s were common. Collectors warn that later owners added some. **PROBABLE** [303-43]

## 11. Station by station

Approximate temperatures at 25,000 ft on a mid-October day: outside air about -35 to -45 °C. The waist and tail are open to the slipstream. The nose and cockpit get some cabin heat.

1. **Pilot (officer, left seat)**: B-6 or A-9 helmet, or a crusher cap with headset. A-10R mask hanging or worn. An A-2 or B-10 over the F-1. B-3 or B-4 vest. QAC harness or B-7 back pack. Hands on the yoke, upright, headphones on.
2. **Co-pilot (officer, right seat)**: the same as the pilot. Often the one wearing the armour in the training film.
3. **Bombardier (officer, nose)**: in the plexiglass nose, kneeling or sitting at the Norden sight or hunched over the chin turret controls (B-17G) or cheek and nose guns (B-17F). A B-6, an A-10R and an F-1 under a B-10 or A-2. Flak vest and apron on for the bomb run. QAC chest pack near his feet. The nose is cold and draughty through the gun openings.
4. **Navigator (officer, nose)**: at his desk on the left side, or on a cheek .50. Dressed like the bombardier, with a flak vest on if available.
5. **Engineer / top turret (enlisted)**: standing in the Sperry upper turret with his head in the dome. A B-6, an A-10R and an F-1 under a B-10. No flak vest. He has the larger walk-around bottle for checking the ship.
6. **Radio operator (enlisted)**: seated at the radio desk, firing the upper .50 through the open hatch. The F-1 under a B-10 or B-3. Flak vest. The hatch makes the compartment cold.
7. **Ball turret (enlisted)**: curled up in the Sperry ball, knees up, sighting between his feet. The F-1 (with first claim on power), a B-6 and an A-10R, no bulky outer layers, no chute and no flak vest. Possibly no harness. Small build.
8. **Left and right waist gunners (enlisted)**: standing at open windows with a .50, braced against the slipstream. The full F-1 + B-3 + A-3 sheepskin set, heated and wool gloves, goggles down, flak vest and apron worn over everything. Coldest positions; frostbite common.
9. **Tail gunner (enlisted)**: kneeling on a pad with the twin .50s. The F-1 + B-3/A-3 or a B-10. Flak vest optional. Very cold and draughty.

## 12. Bodies and faces

- Ages: gunners mostly 19 to 22 and officers about 22 to 25. Secondary sources give averages of about 21 for enlisted and 23 for officers. **PROBABLE**
- Builds: average fitness, about 5 ft 8 in (173 cm). A height and weight cap (70 in / 170 lb) was reported for gunners from January 1943. **UNVERIFIED**
- Ball turret gunners were chosen small. One named example is 5 ft 4 in and 110 lb. **PROBABLE**
- Face coverage: with an A-10R on and goggles down, nothing of the face shows except the bridge of the nose and a strip of skin at the cheekbones. With goggles up on the forehead, the eyes and eyebrows show. The A-14's bigger chin cup hides more. **PROBABLE**

## Biggest modelling errors to avoid

1. **An A-14 mask on everyone.** The A-14 is the classic WWII mask in films and reenactments, but it reached 8th AF bomber crews mainly from early 1944. For October 1943 use the **A-10 Revised**.
2. **A-11 helmets and B-8 goggles everywhere.** Both are mostly 1944. Use the **B-6** (sheepskin) or **A-9**, with **AN6530/B-7 or RAF Mk VII** goggles.
3. **F-2 or F-3 heated suits.** On 14 Oct 1943 the heated suit is the **blue F-1**, worn under sheepskin, a B-10, an A-2 or an A-4. The F-2 was standardized August 1943 but is barely seen in England before 1944.
4. **M3/M4 flak helmets.** Neither was in service yet: the M4 was standardized December 1943, and M3s reached England from about December 1943. At most a few cut-down M1s and the odd Grow helmet.
5. **Red-painted harnesses.** Red marking was required from May 1944. Harnesses in October 1943 are **plain OD**.
6. **Chest packs worn all the time.** Normally the man wears only the QAC harness, with the pack stowed. **The ball gunner has no chute in the turret.**
7. **Flak vests on the top and ball turret gunners.** They had none. Vests were patchy in October 1943 and mostly put on near the target.
8. **The B-17G enclosed waist window.** Closed waist windows are a 1944 change. October 1943 waist gunners stand at **open windows**, in full sheepskin.
9. **Mae West colour and shape.** The B-3/B-4 is a **yellow collar** under the harness, not an orange bulk vest.
10. **Visible rank.** Rank is almost invisible in flight. Don't give enlisted men visible chevrons or officers bars over the Mae West.
